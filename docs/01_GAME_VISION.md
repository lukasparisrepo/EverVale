# 01 — Game Vision

## Fantasy

EverVale should feel like owning a tiny world rather than operating a spreadsheet.

At city scale the player sees networks, production, demand, safety, and expansion. At close zoom the same numbers become visible life: lights turning on, students walking to school, delivery carts moving goods, citizens using parks, workers repairing damage, aircraft taking off.

## Emotional rhythm

The dominant mood is calm competence.

Typical rhythm:
1. observe
2. notice a need
3. build or change a system
4. watch society adapt
5. optimize
6. decorate
7. respond to an unexpected event
8. recover
9. return to calm growth

Threats create contrast, not constant stress.

## Player agency

The player chooses priorities:
- beautiful compact town
- productive industrial settlement
- educated research society
- strong defensive state
- trade-oriented economy
- balanced civic community

Choices should alter the visible form of the world.

## Readability principle

If a system matters, it should have at least one world-space manifestation.

Examples:
- low water → dry fountain / warning markers / idle facilities
- school crowding → visible queues
- logistics bottleneck → waiting vehicles
- low power → dark buildings
- high happiness → parks and plazas visibly used
- threat → scouts, sirens, shelter movement, patrols

## Long-term promise

The player should be able to return after hours or days and ask:

**“What happened while I was gone?”**

The game must answer through a readable after-action timeline and an updated living world.
