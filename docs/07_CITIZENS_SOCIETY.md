# 07 — Citizens & Society

## Design rule

Citizens should **look individual** but the v0.1 economy may remain aggregate.

Do not build a life-simulator before the town loop works.

## Population

Population is generated by:
- Housing capacity
- Happiness
- elapsed time

The player cannot buy citizens directly.

## Workforce

Reference rule:

`Workforce = floor(Population × 0.75)`

The remaining population represents children, dependents and non-working residents abstractly.

This ratio can later be replaced by real demographics without changing the economy contract.

## Job assignment

Workplaces expose job slots.

Workers are auto-assigned based on:
1. criticality
2. player job priority
3. road accessibility
4. commute cost

Player manages priorities rather than dragging individual citizens into rooms.

## Work creates Money

Citizens visibly commute to jobs.

Their assigned workplace determines the economic yield:
- Workshop: high Money
- Utilities: low Money + resource output
- School: low Money + Tech
- Mine: low Money + Varum
- Defense: no Money + safety

This makes Workforce allocation the central economic tradeoff.

## Happiness

Happiness responds to:
- Power
- Water
- Housing
- Employment
- Education
- Recreation
- Safety

A happy town with spare Housing creates Growth.

## Education

School has two functions:
- citywide Education coverage
- Tech Point production

Individual degrees are not required in v0.1.

## Daily visual behavior

Client presentation can show:
- home
- commute
- work
- school activity
- park use
- social idle
- shelter behavior

Logical schedules are server-owned, but exact per-frame walking is client-interpolated.

## Threat behavior

During danger:
- civilians stop normal routines
- seek safe buildings / home / shelter
- defenders deploy
- normal schedules resume after all-clear

## Scale

Server sends logical states and destinations, not every citizen transform at 60 FPS.
