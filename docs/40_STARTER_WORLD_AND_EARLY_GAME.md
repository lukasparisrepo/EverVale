# 40 — Starter World & Early Game

Status: **CANONICAL ONBOARDING BASELINE**

## Design goal

The player starts with a functioning tiny settlement, not an empty field and not a solved economy.

They should immediately understand:
- people live here
- roads matter
- Power/Water matter
- work creates Money
- Happiness affects growth
- expansion requires choices

# Starting population

- Population: 12
- Housing capacity: 12
- Happiness: 80 initial
- Workforce: 9

# Starting buildings

Placed and connected:

1. **Hearth Hall**
2. **3 × Hearth Cottage**
3. **1 × Sunwheel Power Works**
4. **1 × Clearbrook Water Works**
5. **1 × Copperwheel Workshop**
6. **short starter road network**

The town is functional but Housing is full.

First obvious strategic choice:
- build another House to permit Population growth

# Starting resources

Reference:
- Money: 1200
- Power reserve: 60
- Water reserve: 60
- Varum: 0
- Tech: T0 / 0 points

# Starting map

Player begins on Terrace 0.

Visible nearby:
- one cheap rubble blocker
- one forest patch
- one inaccessible higher terrace
- one future road route
- distant hint of Varum-bearing rock

This teaches expansion without a tutorial wall of text.

# First 60–120 minutes intended rhythm

1. inspect living town
2. build House
3. Growth begins
4. choose Park / School / Workshop / Farm / Restaurant
5. utility headroom becomes visible
6. clear first blocker
7. expand road
8. encounter first minor incident
9. unlock T1
10. locate/reach Varum
11. first threat warning/attack occurs within bounded onboarding window

No exact countdown is shown.

# No auto-build

Automation runs citizens and buildings.

Automation does **not** decide to:
- buy new buildings
- clear expansion blockers
- spend Tech unlocks
- produce expensive military units
- redesign roads
- choose expansion territory

Those remain player decisions and are the main anti-idle boundary.
